Differential Learning Rate Training Makes Hybrid Transformers Work


ABSTRACT

We investigate training dynamics for hybrid transformer architectures that combine softmax attention with state space models. Through multi-seed experiments on WikiText-2, we discover that the architecture alone does not explain performance differences. A 4 Softmax + 4 Mamba hybrid trained with uniform learning rates performs significantly worse than a full softmax baseline (191.5 vs 167.3 PPL). However, the same architecture trained with differential learning rates, where each module type leads in turn, achieves 6% better perplexity than the baseline (157.5 vs 167.3). This 34-point improvement comes entirely from the training strategy, not the architecture. We further show that extended training causes rapid overfitting, and the optimal strategy is early stopping during the second training phase rather than adding coordination phases. These findings suggest that training dynamics, not architecture, determine whether hybrid models succeed or fail.


1. INTRODUCTION

Hybrid transformer architectures that combine different sequence modeling mechanisms have shown promise in recent work. Configurations mixing softmax attention with state space models like Mamba, or replacing late attention layers with MLPs, have reported improvements over homogeneous transformers.

However, our multi-seed experiments reveal a surprising finding: the architecture alone does not explain these improvements. The same hybrid architecture can perform significantly worse or significantly better than the baseline depending entirely on the training strategy.

This work makes three contributions.

First, we show that a 4 Softmax + 4 Mamba architecture trained with uniform learning rates performs 14% worse than a full softmax baseline across five random seeds (191.5 vs 167.3 PPL). Claims of architectural improvements based on single runs may reflect lucky seeds rather than genuine benefits.

Second, we demonstrate that differential learning rate training, where softmax and Mamba modules take turns leading with higher learning rates, transforms the same architecture from 14% worse to 6% better than baseline (157.5 PPL). This 34-point improvement comes entirely from training dynamics.

Third, we show that extended training causes rapid overfitting. The optimal strategy is early stopping during the Mamba training phase, not adding coordination phases where both modules train jointly.

Our central thesis is that training dynamics, not architecture, determine whether hybrid models succeed.


2. BACKGROUND

2.1 The Redundancy Bottleneck

When multiple modules can reduce the same training loss, gradient descent allocates explanatory responsibility to the module with the shortest effective path from input to loss. The faster-learning module monopolizes gradient signal, crowding out slower modules before they can specialize.

This phenomenon appears across many settings: MoE routing collapse, multitask interference, lottery ticket competition, and hybrid architecture failures. The common mechanism is learning-speed asymmetry between competing modules.

2.2 State Space Models

Mamba introduces selective state spaces where transition matrices are input-dependent, allowing content-based retention and forgetting. Unlike attention's discrete token selection, Mamba maintains smooth state evolution with linear complexity in sequence length.

2.3 Prior Claims About Hybrid Architectures

Previous work has claimed that hybrid architectures combining softmax attention with Mamba or MLPs can outperform homogeneous transformers. Our initial single-run experiments appeared to confirm this, with a 4 Softmax + 4 Mamba configuration achieving 154.0 PPL compared to 169.2 for full softmax.

However, these claims were based on individual training runs without multi-seed validation.


3. METHODS

3.1 Model Architecture

We use an 8-layer transformer with hidden dimension 512, 8 attention heads, head dimension 64, FFN dimension 2048, vocabulary size 50,257 (GPT-2 tokenizer), and approximately 45 million parameters.

The hybrid configuration uses softmax attention for layers 0-3 and Mamba for layers 4-7. We implement Mamba in pure PyTorch with a Triton-accelerated selective scan kernel.

3.2 Training Strategies

We compare two training strategies for the hybrid architecture.

Uniform Learning Rate: All parameters trained with the same learning rate (1e-4) for 10 epochs. This is the standard approach.

Differential Learning Rate: Parameters grouped by module type with phased training.

Phase 1, Softmax Lead, lasting 4 epochs: Softmax layers receive learning rate 3e-4, Mamba layers receive 1e-5 (minimal but not frozen).

Phase 2, Mamba Catchup, lasting 4-5 epochs: Softmax layers receive 1e-5, Mamba layers receive 3e-4.

The key insight is that we do not add a coordination phase where both modules train at equal rates. Our experiments show this causes degradation.

3.3 Evaluation

We train on WikiText-2 with sequence length 512 and batch size 8. We report perplexity on the validation set. All experiments use 5 random seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 1337) to establish statistical reliability.


4. EXPERIMENTS

4.1 Multi-Seed Validation

We run each configuration with 5 different random seeds to measure true performance and variance.

Full Softmax baseline results across seeds: 168.2, 167.2, 167.6, 165.4, 168.2. Mean 167.3, standard deviation 1.1.

4 Softmax + 4 Mamba with uniform LR results across seeds: 189.7, 197.6, 187.8, 187.4, 195.1. Mean 191.5, standard deviation 4.3.

4 Softmax + 4 Mamba with differential LR results across seeds: 158.4, 159.1, 157.8, 156.0, 156.2. Mean 157.5, standard deviation 1.3.

These results reveal three critical findings.

First, the hybrid architecture with uniform training is significantly worse than baseline. The 191.5 mean PPL is 14% worse than the 167.3 baseline. This contradicts claims that hybrid architectures inherently improve performance.

Second, differential LR training transforms the same architecture from worse to better. The 157.5 mean PPL is 6% better than baseline. The architecture is identical; only the training strategy changed.

Third, differential LR training reduces variance. The standard deviation drops from 4.3 (uniform) to 1.3 (differential), suggesting more stable optimization.

4.2 The Lucky Seed Problem

Our initial single-run experiment reported 154.0 PPL for the hybrid architecture, suggesting a 9% improvement over baseline. This result was misleading.

With multi-seed validation, we find that 154.0 was likely a lucky seed. The true mean with uniform training is 191.5, which is 14% worse than baseline. Single-run results in this domain are unreliable.

4.3 Extended Training and Overfitting

We investigate the training dynamics in detail by tracking per-epoch performance during differential LR training.

Softmax Lead phase results by epoch: Epoch 1 achieves 499.6 PPL, Epoch 2 achieves 347.0, Epoch 3 achieves 278.5, Epoch 4 achieves 234.6.

Mamba Catchup phase results by epoch: Epoch 5 achieves 187.9 PPL, Epoch 6 achieves 170.8, Epoch 7 achieves 162.3, Epoch 8 achieves 158.4, Epoch 9 achieves 187.2.

The critical observation is that PPL improves steadily through epoch 8 (158.4) then degrades sharply at epoch 9 (187.2). This 29-point degradation occurs within the same training phase, before any coordination.

Extended training causes overfitting. The optimal strategy is early stopping at epoch 8.

4.4 Coordination Phase Ablation

We test whether adding a coordination phase after Mamba Catchup can improve results. Starting from the epoch 9 checkpoint (187.2 PPL), we train with both modules at equal learning rates.

Results with different coordination learning rates:

No coordination achieves best PPL of 158.4 at epoch 8, with final PPL of 187.2 at epoch 9.

Coordination with LR 1e-4 recovers to best PPL of 174.5.

Coordination with LR 5e-5 recovers to best PPL of 175.8.

Coordination with LR 1e-5 recovers to best PPL of 176.2.

Coordination with LR 5e-6 recovers to best PPL of 176.9.

Coordination with LR 1e-6 recovers to best PPL of 179.5.

Coordination can partially recover from overfitting (187.2 down to 174.5 at best). However, no coordination learning rate beats the early stopping result of 158.4.

The optimal strategy is to stop training at the right time, not to add recovery phases.


5. ANALYSIS

5.1 Why Does Uniform Training Fail?

With uniform learning rates, softmax attention learns faster than Mamba due to simpler gradient paths. The softmax layers quickly capture most of the learnable signal, leaving Mamba layers undertrained.

This is the redundancy bottleneck in action. Both module types can reduce the same loss, so the faster learner dominates. The Mamba layers become dead weight, making the hybrid worse than a pure softmax model that at least uses all its parameters effectively.

5.2 Why Does Differential Training Succeed?

Differential LR training breaks the competition by giving each module a protected learning window.

During Softmax Lead, attention layers learn to route information and extract features while Mamba layers make minimal updates (maintaining compatibility but not competing).

During Mamba Catchup, Mamba layers learn to process the structured representations created by softmax, while attention layers hold steady.

This phased approach allows both modules to specialize rather than compete. The result is better than either module alone.

5.3 Why Does Extended Training Hurt?

The sharp degradation from epoch 8 (158.4) to epoch 9 (187.2) shows that the model rapidly overfits once both modules are reasonably trained. Additional training does not help because the modules begin interfering with each other's learned representations.

This explains why coordination phases fail to beat early stopping. Joint training reintroduces the competition that differential LR was designed to avoid.

5.4 Implications for Architecture Claims

Many reported improvements from hybrid architectures may actually be training artifacts. Without multi-seed validation and careful attention to training dynamics, it is easy to mistake a lucky seed or favorable training trajectory for an architectural improvement.

Our results suggest that the question "which architecture is better?" may be less important than "how should this architecture be trained?"


6. PRACTICAL RECOMMENDATIONS

Based on our validated findings, we recommend the following.

For hybrid Softmax + Mamba architectures, always use differential learning rate training. Uniform training makes the hybrid worse than baseline.

The optimal training schedule is 4 epochs of Softmax Lead with softmax LR at 3e-4 and Mamba LR at 1e-5, followed by 4 epochs of Mamba Catchup with softmax LR at 1e-5 and Mamba LR at 3e-4.

Use early stopping based on validation perplexity. In our experiments, epoch 8 was optimal. Do not extend training hoping for further improvement.

Do not add coordination phases. Even with carefully tuned learning rates, coordination cannot beat early stopping.

Always validate with multiple seeds. Single-run results in this domain are unreliable. We observed a 34-point PPL difference between training strategies for identical architectures.


7. LIMITATIONS

Our experiments use approximately 45 million parameter models on WikiText-2. Scaling behavior to larger models and datasets is unknown. The optimal phase lengths and learning rates likely depend on model size.

We did not complete multi-seed validation for the 6 Softmax + 2 MLP configuration due to a technical issue. Claims about MLP replacement require separate validation.

The Triton kernel performance is hardware-dependent. We used an RTX 4090; results will vary on other GPUs.


8. RELATED WORK

The redundancy bottleneck phenomenon appears in multiple contexts. Mixture of Experts models suffer from routing collapse where a few experts dominate. Multi-task learning shows interference between tasks with different learning speeds. Lottery ticket hypothesis work shows that subnetworks compete during training.

Our differential LR approach is related to curriculum learning, where training proceeds in phases of increasing difficulty. However, our phases are defined by module type rather than data difficulty.

Mamba and other state space models have been proposed as attention replacements. Our work suggests that the integration strategy matters as much as the module choice.


9. CONCLUSION

We demonstrate that training dynamics, not architecture, determine whether hybrid transformers succeed. A 4 Softmax + 4 Mamba architecture performs 14% worse than baseline with uniform training but 6% better with differential learning rate training. This 34-point improvement comes entirely from giving each module type a protected learning window.

Our multi-seed experiments reveal that single-run results are unreliable. Previous claims of architectural improvements may reflect lucky seeds rather than genuine benefits.

The optimal training strategy is phased differential learning rates with early stopping. Extended training and coordination phases cause degradation rather than improvement.

These findings have broad implications for hybrid architecture research. When evaluating novel combinations of modules, researchers should carefully control for training dynamics and validate with multiple seeds. The question of how to train may matter more than what to train.
